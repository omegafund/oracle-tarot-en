I Ching Hexagram 55: Feng · Abundance

From the author of Business I Ching · Classical text with a modern leadership reading

豐
Number
55 of 64
Name
豐 Feng — Abundance
Korean reading
Noehwa Pung (뇌화풍)
Upper trigram
☳ Thunder (Zhen)
Lower trigram
☲ Fire (Li)
Keywords
Peak, fullness, power, brilliance, the turn after midday
Short answerHexagram 55 is the moment of fullness: thunder above, fire below, everything bright and powerful. It says do not be sad — be like the sun at midday. But the commentary adds the hard truth: once the sun reaches noon, it begins to decline.

Feng describes a person, company or nation at the height of its power. Thunder brings movement, fire brings clarity, and together they create a period of brilliant success.

The I Ching does not celebrate this moment naively. Its commentary reminds us that the full moon wanes and the midday sun starts to set. Abundance is not a place to rest; it is the most delicate stage of all.

The judgment and the image

The judgment: abundance brings success; the king attains it; do not grieve — be like the sun at midday. The commentary explains the warning hidden inside: when the sun is at its height it begins to sink, and when the moon is full it begins to wane (日中則昃，月盈則食). The image — thunder and lightning arriving together — asks the noble person to judge clearly and act decisively.

The business lesson

In Business I Ching, this chapter is titled “America at Its Peak and the Accumulating Power of China.” Kwang-tae Eum does not simply declare the United States the strongest nation; he reads its finance, technology, AI, universities and military as a state of Feng — and asks what a nation at the peak must prepare while it is still there. The lesson applies to any market leader: the greatest danger of abundance is the curtain it draws over your own eyes, the darkness at noon described in the lines.

The six lines: stages of the situation

  • Line 1 — Meeting a partner of equal strength. Alliances formed in abundance bring no blame.
  • Line 2 — The curtain is so thick that stars are seen at noon. Success breeds suspicion; win trust through sincerity.
  • Line 3 — Darkness thickens; the right arm is broken. A key capability is lost — no blame if you accept it.
  • Line 4 — Stars at noon, yet a like-minded ally appears. Find the people who still see clearly.
  • Line 5 — Bright talents are drawn in. Blessing and praise come from gathering excellent people.
  • Line 6 — A grand house, but the family is screened off. Peering through the gate: empty for three years. Isolation at the top.

In career, money, relationships & timing

Career

You may be at a high point. Use it to bring in talent and fix weaknesses, not to coast.

Money

Peak conditions: lock in gains, diversify, and watch for signs the cycle is turning.

Relationships

Success can isolate. Keep honest voices close and stay reachable.

Timing

Midday — act decisively now, while preparing for the afternoon that follows.

The question this hexagram asks

“At the height of my success, what am I no longer able to see?”

Frequently asked questions

What does I Ching Hexagram 55 mean?

Hexagram 55, Feng or Abundance, describes a peak of power and success, with the warning that every peak is followed by a turn — the sun at midday begins to set.

Is Hexagram 55 a good or bad sign?

It signals success, but asks for vigilance. The best response is to act decisively and generously while you are strong, rather than assume the peak will last.

What does Hexagram 55 mean in business?

It is the hexagram of market leaders and superpowers. In Business I Ching it is read through America at its peak. Its lesson is that abundance can blind leadership; the antidote is gathering capable people and keeping clear sight.

What are the trigrams of Hexagram 55?

Thunder (Zhen) above and Fire (Li) below — movement combined with clarity, like thunder and lightning arriving together.
